# Character asset authoring

The asset boundary accepts two deliberately small Blender-to-GLB paths: a baked
static world mesh and a one-skin character with a bounded transform-clip catalog.
These import reusable assets. Compose their supported placements in the
[scene editor](scene-editor.md); a GLB does not define the playable scene.

## Blender convention

Author in metres using Blender's native Z-up coordinates, with the character
facing Blender `-Y`. Blender's exporter converts that to the runtime contract:
Y up and `+Z` forward. Keep each static asset as one mesh object, one material and one
embedded RGBA PNG base-colour texture. Apply object transforms when they are
part of the mesh; ordinary parent node translation, rotation and scale are also
accepted and baked once at import.

The material must be opaque and have **Backface Culling** enabled. Use one UV
map and the default repeat-wrapped Image Texture node connected to Principled
BSDF Base Color. Scalar Metallic and Roughness values are accepted but
deliberately normalized away: the renderer uses lit base colour and a shared
wet sheen in storm scenes rather than authored PBR surface response. Normal, occlusion, emissive and metallic-roughness
textures remain unsupported and are rejected.

Export as glTF 2.0 with:

- Format: **glTF Binary (`.glb`)**
- Include: the selected mesh only, with cameras and lights disabled
- Data: UVs and normals enabled; animations, skinning and shape keys absent
- Images: PNG

Blender's GLB exporter writes a trilinear sampler. The importer validates that
request and builds the complete mip chain in linear colour space; the GPU then
samples it through an sRGB texture. This avoids shimmering and preserves colour
when the pulled-out camera reduces many texels into one.

## Reproducible compatibility fixture

The checked-in `assets/fixtures/blender-static-preview.glb` is generated by
Blender rather than assembled by the engine's diagnostic fixture writer. From
the repository root:

```text
blender --background --python assets/fixtures/generate-blender-static-preview.py
```

The script uses ordinary mesh objects, UV maps, a Principled material, a packed
PNG and Blender's normal GLB exporter. The resulting asymmetric, metre-scale
training dummy is the authoring-workflow gate. The hand-written
`static-preview.glb` remains the smaller arithmetic and orientation diagnostic.

## Bind-pose character

The skinned path keeps the same unit, facing, material, UV and texture rules,
but every mesh must be controlled by the same armature. Give every retained object
and bone a non-empty, unique name. A character may retain multiple mesh objects
and multiple material primitives within each mesh. They share one rooted node
tree and one skin of at most 64 joints, with exactly four joint-index and weight
lanes per vertex. Zero-weight lanes are fine; the nonzero weights must be
finite and sum to a positive value. The importer normalizes them once.

Parts preserve their mesh node name, primitive index and material assignment.
Each material has its own base-colour factor and embedded RGBA PNG; materials
may share an image, which is decoded and uploaded only once. The importer checks
aggregate geometry and texture memory before accepting the complete character.
The part/catalog and decoded-texture budgets are defined in
[`character.rs`](../crates/assets/src/character.rs); per-image and total geometry
limits live in [`assets`](../crates/assets/src/lib.rs). Unused catalog entries,
extra skins and unsupported material features are rejected, including in later
parts. A refused load leaves the active character intact.

All parts use the same sampled pose. Clothing can bend with the body, while a
sword can follow one hand by weighting it to a weapon bone. Each material
primitive adds a draw call per character pose; horde instances still share the
same bounded pose buckets. This establishes separate authored parts within one
GLB. Runtime equipment selection and loading items from separate files are not
implemented yet.

Apply each mesh object's location, rotation and scale before export so every
mesh's global node transform is identity. Bone transforms remain authored hierarchy
data and must not be applied to the mesh. This division is deliberate: the skeleton
produces asset-local poses, while the app's `Instance` is the only placement,
scale and facing transform used by the renderer.

Export all selected character meshes and their armature together as a binary GLB
with UVs, normals, skinning and Actions animation export enabled. Keep shape keys,
cameras, lights and glTF extensions disabled. Blender must emit inverse bind matrices;
the importer rejects a skin whose bind-pose palette does not reproduce every
authored vertex.

Each action must have a non-empty unique name, two to 256 keys per channel, a
duration no longer than 60 seconds, and matching start/end times across every
channel. The emitted glTF may use `LINEAR` or `STEP`; Blender commonly exports
motion as `LINEAR` and unchanged components as `STEP`. Cubic splines, morph
targets, non-joint targets and non-uniform scale are rejected explicitly. Key
the bind pose at both ends when the clip should loop without a seam. One asset
may contain at most 16 clips.

The player presentation contract resolves `Idle`, `Run`, `RunBackward`,
`RunLeft`, `RunRight`, `AttackCleave`, `AttackSlam` and `Disintegrate` once at load.
The player also requires a `BeamOrigin` joint at the sword tip. Presentation
reads its sampled transform to attach the beam without changing collision geometry.
Locomotion clips share a normalized gait phase and blend according to actual
displacement relative to facing. The attack layer validates the articulated chest, shoulder, arm, hand and weapon
chain at load; its exact mask is owned by `player_upper_mask` in
[`presentation.rs`](../crates/app/src/presentation.rs). Moving melee retains
pelvis and leg locomotion; stationary melee uses the complete bracing pose.
Moving ability layers align their top masked joint to the source parent's
rotation, so turning strafe hips cannot turn a committed swing or beam off aim.
The validated, allocation-free `blend_parent_aligned` operation belongs to the
asset pose owner; ordinary locomotion still blends local transforms.
The mask is validated before replacing a live character; only articulated body
and weapon joints are required. Attack actions reserve consecutive authored
segments for wind-up, contact and recovery; app maps those segments to the
committed simulation phase lengths. Recovery tuning therefore cannot move the
visible contact interval. These names and segment boundaries are
gameplay-presentation roles owned by `app`, not special cases in the importer.

The playable player attacks use the same 30-frame authoring contract: frame 1 is
neutral, frame 13 is fully wound, frame 19 completes contact, and frame 31 is
neutral again. Runtime maps startup to normalized `0.0..0.4`, active to
`0.4..0.6`, and recovery to `0.6..1.0`; tests sample the exported weapon motion
at those edges so moving a key pose into the wrong phase fails visibly.

The swordsman player uses a 26-joint rig fitted to the accepted CC0 authored
human surface, with close-fitting clothing and a rigid sword,
and a Cleave retargeted from approved authored motion, including weight transfer
and an articulated wrist. [Motion acceptance](character-motion.md) records the
visual principles. Authoring exports ordinary transform clips; runtime uses the
same importer and pose sampler.

The player sword is part of its single mesh and is rigidly
weighted to its `Weapon` bone. This preserves the full sampled transform through
the existing skinning path. The importer requires named nodes, but a generic
character GLB is not required to contain a joint called `Weapon`.

The ordinary-horde contract is a smaller view of the same interchange format:
it requires `Idle`, `Run`, `Hit` and `Death`. Three fixed hit poses share the
recoil stages, and one frozen death pose supports a tick-derived fall. `horde show <path.glb>` selects one shared
character, including all its parts and materials, then groups live enemies into
bounded [gait/phase buckets](crowd-collision.md#movement-and-pushing). Phase comes
from stable entity identity, not authored or stored randomness. Startup loads `assets/characters/basic-player/basic-player.glb`
for the player and `assets/characters/basic-enemy/basic-enemy.glb` for the horde,
using the existing role scales and tints. Asset paths
are resolved from the build-time repository location, independently of the launch
directory. Missing or invalid defaults fail startup explicitly. `character clear`
and `horde clear` restore their respective default assets; there is no character cube fallback.

An ordinary horde asset may have a neighboring `<stem>.shadow.glb` file. Both
the harness and companion load the complete pair before replacing the accepted
horde. A present but unreadable, invalid or mismatched caster refuses the load;
an asset without a caster uses its visible geometry for shadows. The asset owner
requires fewer caster triangles and the same ordered joint names, joint parents,
local/global bind transforms and inverse binds. The renderer uses only its
geometry with the visible asset's palette; proxy clips/materials are not uploaded.
Visible geometry, materials and animation are unchanged. The horde report's
`shadow_mesh` object identifies which geometry is active. Scene restart clears
placements and poses through the existing horde lifetime; changing assets replaces
the complete GPU resources, including the caster.

The checked-in `assets/fixtures/blender-bind-pose.glb` exercises this contract
with a four-bone, asymmetric training dummy and basic animation clips.
It remains an importer/horde fixture; the playable player is the complete
directional-locomotion and upper-body-layer fixture.
Its diagnostic motion is distinct from the playable player's attack authoring.
Regenerate it from the repository root with:

```text
blender --background --python assets/fixtures/generate-blender-bind-pose.py
```

The multipart fixture compares the same training-dummy geometry as one mesh
and as five meshes, using three independent colour images and one shared rig.
Importer tests compare animated triangles; GPU tests require identical images
through both player and instanced horde rendering. Regenerate both files with:

```text
blender --background --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 \
  --python assets/fixtures/generate-blender-multipart.py
```

The basic placeholder player and its editable Blender source live in
[`assets/characters/basic-player`](../assets/characters/basic-player/README.md).
The dedicated horde asset lives in
[`assets/characters/basic-enemy`](../assets/characters/basic-enemy/README.md).
Each generator produces its checked-in `.blend` and `.glb` artifacts.
